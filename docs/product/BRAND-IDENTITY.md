# Brand Identity

## Strategic direction
Blend two qualities: **precision of a premium camera instrument** and **imagination of a futuristic creative technology studio**. Creative technology leads the emotional tone; photographic precision earns trust.

## Working name
LumaLab is the repository/product working name only. It is **not cleared for public launch** and must remain provisional. A public trademark search surfaced a live/pending international LUMA LAB application owned by Luma Stiftung (serial 79407426), with listed services including computer software design/development and education-related services in classes 42 and 41 in the United States. This is a material naming-collision signal for a software-and-learning product, though it is not an exact goods/services match. Reference: https://trademarks.justia.com/794/07/luma-79407426.html.

The Indian Trade Marks Registry provides an official existing-mark search at https://ipindia.gov.in/trade-marks-before-you-apply-search-existing-trademarks. Its dynamic registry search was not completed in this pass. No legal opinion, worldwide clearance, domain availability or right-to-use conclusion is claimed.

**Decision:** retain Lumalab as the repository identifier to avoid unnecessary repo churn, but do not commit to LumaLab as the public product brand, purchase branding assets or launch under this name. Shortlist a more distinctive coined name and obtain professional clearance in India and intended export markets before public use.

## Positioning statement
A virtual photography studio where people explore light, optics and image-making by doing—not just reading.

## Brand promise
Understand light. Master the image.

## Personality
- Precise, not clinical.
- Cinematic, not theatrical.
- Intelligent, not intimidating.
- Experimental, not gimmicky.
- Premium, not exclusive.

## Visual principles
1. The photograph/3D scene is the hero; UI chrome stays quiet.
2. Use deep neutral surfaces to make light, glass and image previews stand out.
3. Electric cool blue signals controls, focus and technical selection.
4. Warm amber is reserved for light temperature, exposure warmth and creative emphasis.
5. Fine measurement marks and optical diagrams add instrument credibility.
6. Avoid generic AI gradients, excessive glassmorphism, neon overload and decorative camera clichés.

## Typography
Use a neutral modern sans (Inter or metrically compatible alternative) for UI. Use tabular numerals for camera readouts. A restrained display face may be used for marketing headlines only; never sacrifice instrument readability.

## Voice
Short, clear, confident, curious. Explain technical terms in context. Prefer “Move focus to 2.5 m” over vague copy such as “Make it pop”. Never overstate optical accuracy.

## Logo direction
Develop a compact optical-aperture / focus-plane mark that remains legible at 16–24 px. Avoid literal camera silhouettes and overly complex aperture blades. Wordmark should work in monochrome and on dark/light backgrounds.

## Tagline
Primary: “Understand light. Master the image.”
Supporting: “A virtual studio for the science and art of photography.”

## Brand gates before public launch
- Complete exact and phonetic searches in IP India for relevant Nice classes (especially 9, 41 and 42), plus WIPO and relevant target-market registries.
- Check company names, app stores, domains and common-law use for exact, spaced, phonetic and confusingly similar variants.
- Obtain a qualified trademark professional's written clearance opinion before adoption or filing.
- Test pronunciation and recall with beginner and professional audiences.
- Produce logo masters and app icons only after name selection.
- Validate contrast and small-size legibility.
